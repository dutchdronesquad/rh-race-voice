# Busy-race synthesis limits

Lap preparation keeps at most four pending callouts plus one active job. A newer lap for the same pilot replaces pending older speech and cancels preparation of that pilot's older lap unless its cached prefix has already been published for playback. A published prefix retains its callout slot until its time is appended or a signal, stop, failure or expiry invalidates it. An operation already running in Piper may finish, but its obsolete result cannot reach playback. Expired or cancelled requests are removed from the worker queue when their last subscriber leaves, immediately freeing capacity for fresh speech. Race signals and other voice announcements are outside the lap limit.

Piper synthesis and ONNX model loading run in one persistent subprocess outside RotorHazard and the service's event loop, with at most two ONNX compute threads (one on a single- or dual-core host). The pool uses a 1 ms spin window and exponential backoff to reduce idle CPU use; see [ONNX thread management](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/performance/tune-performance/threading.html). All segments of a live callout are submitted together at the same priority, then generated serially by the existing worker. This prevents queued, lower-priority pre-cache work from starting between the name, lap number and time. An already running background operation must still finish. Preparation remains manual; prepare audio before racing. The preparation button explicitly warms the model even when every phrase is already cached.

The supervisor retains up to 128 reusable segments and 8 MiB of immutable WAV bytes in an LRU RAM cache. Prepared names, lap numbers and countdown phrases can be fetched without using the inference queue or rereading WAV files. Model/config metadata is checked off the event loop before reuse; different text or voice settings have separate identities. Full cache clears and worker failures discard resident results, while heat cleanup retains reusable phrases. Dynamic lap times still use the worker's temporary file cache and do not displace these reusable RAM entries.

The active heat's callsigns and prepared lap numbers take priority over other RAM entries. A heat or voice-setting change updates that preference without synthesizing or clearing audio. When protected entries fill the cache, unrelated phrases remain available through the worker/disk path instead of displacing them. The 128-entry/8 MiB bounds still apply even if a heat alone exceeds them; such a heat may not have every segment resident simultaneously. Switching back to a previously prepared heat can reuse audio that is still resident, and **Prepare pre-cache** restores missing entries explicitly.

Readiness remains internal. Worker replies track whether the inference session is loaded for the selected model/config revision, independently of whether WAVs exist. The early cached-prefix path requires both resident audio and a warm, live worker; a cold model waits for complete preparation so its startup does not leave a long pause between the callsign and time. Rechecking warmth uses the metadata check already performed for RAM hits and starts no extra worker job. After explicit preparation, the service logs `model_warm`, resident `callsigns` and `lap_numbers` counts and `early_lap_speech_ready`; incomplete readiness is logged as a warning. This check also starts no synthesis or warmup, and adds no UI status or periodic polling. Readiness covers the six standard prepared lap numbers and does not guarantee player or audible latency.

Each output also limits waiting lap audio to four callouts and normally four seconds of WAV duration. When either limit is exceeded, the oldest pending lap is discarded, keeping recent callouts in passage order. One complete callout longer than four seconds is allowed on its own. These limits exclude the active callout, other announcements, synthesis time and player buffering; they do not guarantee a maximum audible delay. Already playing lap speech is not interrupted by a new lap. A slow destination has its own queue and cannot delay other outputs.

The overlay can display a lap as soon as the event arrives; speech additionally waits for synthesis, queued playback and client buffering. INFO telemetry records `synthesis_start.wait_ms` for the preparation queue, per-segment cache hits (including `memory_cache_hit`) and generation time, and `playback_start.wait_ms`/`duration_ms` for the output queue and WAV duration. The Sendspin playback log records the planned start delay after client lead and the existing stream timeline are considered. These values help separate preparation, output backlog and scheduling delay; they do not measure the moment sound leaves the speaker. See [latency validation](latency-validation.md) and [local efficiency measurements](efficiency.md). Hardware latency and Raspberry Pi capacity still require measurement on the race setup.
