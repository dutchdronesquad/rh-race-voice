# Busy-race synthesis limits

Lap preparation keeps at most four pending callouts plus one active job. A newer lap for the same pilot replaces pending older speech and cancels preparation of that pilot's older lap. An operation already running in Piper may finish, but its obsolete result cannot reach playback. Expired or cancelled requests are removed from the worker queue when their last subscriber leaves, immediately freeing capacity for fresh speech. Race signals and other voice announcements are outside the lap limit.

Piper synthesis and ONNX model loading run in one persistent subprocess outside RotorHazard and the service's event loop, with at most two ONNX compute threads (one on a single- or dual-core host). All segments of a live callout are submitted together at the same priority, then generated serially by the existing worker. This prevents queued, lower-priority pre-cache work from starting between the name, lap number and time. An already running background operation must still finish. Preparation remains manual; prepare audio before racing.

Each output also limits waiting lap audio to four callouts and normally four seconds of WAV duration. When either limit is exceeded, the oldest pending lap is discarded, keeping recent callouts in passage order. One complete callout longer than four seconds is allowed on its own. These limits exclude the active callout, other announcements, synthesis time and player buffering; they do not guarantee a maximum audible delay. Already playing lap speech is not interrupted by a new lap. A slow destination has its own queue and cannot delay other outputs.

The overlay can display a lap as soon as the event arrives; speech additionally waits for synthesis, queued playback and client buffering. INFO telemetry records `synthesis_start.wait_ms` for the preparation queue, per-segment cache hits and generation time, and `playback_start.wait_ms`/`duration_ms` for the output queue and WAV duration. The Sendspin playback log records the planned start delay after client lead and the existing stream timeline are considered. These values help separate preparation, output backlog and scheduling delay; they do not measure the moment sound leaves the speaker. See [latency validation](latency-validation.md). Hardware latency and Raspberry Pi capacity still require measurement on the race setup.
